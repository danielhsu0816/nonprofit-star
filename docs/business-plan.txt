Nonprofit Discovery Platform — Business Plan
Nov 22, 2026 · @Bonnie
Summary
We are building a free directory that matches people with nonprofits they can volunteer for, donate to or work for, filtered by area and cause. Nonprofits pay us to be seen: a free basic listing, a paid featured spot on the first page, a premium plan with visitor analytics and advice on getting found by AI search, and $50 promotion of their upcoming events in our weekly regional email newsletter and on our homepage.
Our edge is AEO/GEO (answer and generative engine optimization). We build every page so that ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and classic search can read and quote it. So when someone asks "where can I volunteer with animals in Phoenix this weekend?", the answer points to us.
Working name: CauseConnect (placeholder until the team picks one).
Problem
The US has about 1.9 million registered nonprofits (Candid), and most are too small to be found online. People who want to help often can't find a good match nearby.
For nonprofits: most run with tiny teams and little marketing money. Industry surveys report that 63% have 2–10 staff, 41% spend under $25,000 a year on marketing, and 52% say marketing is underfunded (Gitnux, 2026). Big national charities win the search results, while local food banks, shelters and cleanup groups stay invisible.
For people who want to help: demand is there. 75.7 million Americans volunteered formally in 2023, the largest two-year rise AmeriCorps has recorded (AmeriCorps). But finding a local opportunity that fits your cause, area and schedule still means searching many separate sites.
A new reason to give in 2026: starting with tax year 2026, people who take the standard deduction can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts to qualifying charities, or $2,000 for married couples filing jointly (IRS). Kiplinger estimates about 144 million taxpayers could qualify (Kiplinger). Many don't know yet, and they'll need to find eligible charities.
Search is changing: more people now ask AI assistants instead of searching Google. Small nonprofits have no plan for being recommended by AI, and nobody is building the local, structured data that AI tools need in order to recommend them.
Solution and user flow
A visitor goes from landing to a matched nonprofit in under a minute, without making an account.
	•	Intent pop-up. On first visit, a short pop-up asks what they're here for and their emails and name: volunteer, donate, or work for a nonprofit (they can pick more than one).
	•	Area and cause filters. They choose a location (city, ZIP or "near me") and one or more causes: education, food security, environment, animals, health, housing, arts and others.
	•	Recommendations. We rank nonprofits by how well they match the intent, area and cause. Featured (paid) listings show first, clearly labeled "Featured."
	•	Browse freely. Every other matching nonprofit is still shown below, and visitors can change filters or click any option they like.
	•	Link out. Buttons on each listing go to the nonprofit's own volunteer, donation or careers page. We don't handle money or applications.
	•	Tax-deductible badge. Listings verified as 501(c)(3) show a badge, plus a short note: from tax year 2026, people who take the standard deduction can deduct up to $1,000 in cash gifts ($2,000 for joint filers). Itemizers deduct as before, above a floor of 0.5% of income. We give general information, not tax advice.
Each click and each pop-up answer is recorded by our backstage. That data powers the premium analytics nonprofits pay for.
Weekly regional events newsletter: the pop-up includes an opt-in checkbox ("Send me upcoming events near me"). Subscribers are matched by area only: everyone who picked Arizona gets the full Arizona issue, covering every cause and type of event, and nobody outside Arizona gets it. Each issue lists upcoming nonprofit events in that area for the coming week or two, such as volunteer days, fundraisers, cleanups and drives, each with the date, place and a link to sign up or buy tickets. Sponsored events appear first and are clearly labeled, and they also show at the top of the website's first page for that area during the same week.
Nonprofit side: an organization signs up, fills in a profile (mission, causes, locations, links, photos), and we check its EIN against the IRS list before it goes live.
AEO/GEO strategy
AI search is our main free traffic channel, and every new listing makes it stronger. More and more people ask an AI assistant instead of typing keywords into Google, and those assistants quote pages that are clearly structured and directly answer a question.
What we build into the site:
	•	One page per nonprofit, per city × cause, and per city × intent (for example "Volunteer for food banks in Tucson"). Pages are generated from the database, so 1,000 nonprofits can produce tens of thousands of answerable pages.
	•	Structured data (schema.org): NGO, Organization, Event, VolunteerAction and FAQPage markup, so AI systems and search engines read facts (location, cause, how to help) without guessing.
	•	Answer-first writing: each page opens with a one- or two-sentence direct answer, followed by short FAQ blocks ("Are donations to X tax-deductible?", "Do I need experience to volunteer?").
	•	llms.txt and a clean sitemap that point AI crawlers to our most useful pages.
	•	Freshness: nonprofits update events and needs, so pages change often, which search systems reward.
	•	Trust signals: verified EIN, last-updated dates and links to official sources.
How we measure it: referral traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and similar sources; how often our pages are cited when we test common prompts; and organic search impressions.
Why it doubles as a product: the same tracking shows each nonprofit how AI search sees it. Premium members get a report and a fix list for their own website. That turns our growth method into revenue.
Business model
Nonprofits are the paying customer. Visitors always use the site for free. We charge a monthly subscription for visibility and insight. We don't take a cut of donations, so the incentives stay clean.

Go-to-market
We fill the supply side first: nonprofits, through direct email. Then AI and organic search bring visitors at almost no cost.
	•	Build the contact list. Use public sources (the IRS list of tax-exempt organizations and nonprofits' own websites) to find general contact emails, starting with a few launch metros so local pages fill up quickly.
	•	Email campaign. A short, personal email: "Get found by people searching for ways to help in [city]. Free listing, plus one month of Premium free during launch." Include an unsubscribe link and a real address, as US anti-spam rules (CAN-SPAM) require.
	•	Easy onboarding. Signing up takes about 5 minutes: enter the EIN, and we prefill name and address from public data.
	•	Show value in month one. The free Premium month sends weekly emails like "23 people viewed your profile; 6 clicked Volunteer." That becomes the reason to keep paying.
	•	Visitor growth. Every new listing creates new answer pages for AI and organic search (see AEO/GEO strategy). We add short social posts during giving seasons, especially near year-end, when people look for tax-deductible gifts.
	•	Partnerships. Local volunteer centers, universities (service-learning hours) and employers' volunteer programs can point people to the site.

Impact on community and environment
Every match we make sends time, money or talent to a local nonprofit that otherwise wouldn't get it.
	•	Small nonprofits get seen. A free listing and AI-ready pages give a five-person food pantry the same chance to be recommended as a national brand.
	•	More people take action. Lowering the effort from "search ten sites" to "one pop-up and a click" turns intent into real volunteer hours and donations.
	•	More donations from the 2026 tax change. We tell everyday donors about the new deduction for non-itemizers and point them to verified charities.
	•	Talent for the sector. Job seekers find mission-driven roles, which helps understaffed nonprofits hire.
	•	Environment and community as a focus. We highlight environmental and community causes on the home page and in seasonal campaigns (Earth Day, cleanup weeks), and we can commit a share of profits or free Premium seats to environmental nonprofits.
What we'll measure: volunteer, donate and job clicks sent to nonprofits per month; number of nonprofits receiving traffic; share of traffic going to small organizations (for example under $1M revenue); and follow-up surveys of nonprofits on new volunteers and donors gained.
Scalability
Serving 100× more visitors costs us only a little more, because the product is a self-serve directory of mostly static pages.
	•	Self-serve supply. Nonprofits sign up and edit their own profiles, and verification against public IRS data is automated. Each new listing needs no staff time beyond spot checks.
	•	Cheap pages at scale. Pages are generated from the database and served from a CDN, so 10,000 pages cost about the same as 100 to host.
	•	Free acquisition that compounds. Each listing adds city × cause × intent pages that AI and organic search can surface. More listings bring more visitors, more visitors make listings more valuable, and that brings more paying nonprofits.
	•	Analytics are automatic. Dashboards come from the same click logs for every member. The GEO report is generated by a template plus AI, then lightly reviewed.
	•	Reach. The model works in any US city from day one, since the filters are location-based. Expanding to other countries mainly needs each country's charity registry for verification.
The main cost that grows with customers is support and sales staff, and self-serve onboarding and billing keep that low.

Submission text (draft)
Problem: About 1.9 million US nonprofits compete for attention, and most small ones can't afford marketing. Meanwhile, millions of people want to volunteer, donate (especially with the new 2026 deduction for non-itemizers) or work for a cause, but can't easily find local matches.
Solution: CauseConnect asks visitors what they want to do, filters by area and cause, recommends matching nonprofits, and links straight to their volunteer, donate or careers pages. Every page is built for AI search (AEO/GEO), so assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity can recommend local nonprofits through us.
How it makes money: nonprofits list for free, pay $29/month for featured placement, or $79/month for Premium analytics plus an AI-search visibility report. They can also pay $50 per issue to promote an upcoming event in our weekly regional events newsletter (sent only to opted-in subscribers in that area) and on our homepage for that area. Early sign-ups get one month of Premium free. Main costs are hosting, email outreach, AI usage for reports and, later, support staff; all grow far more slowly than users.
Open questions for the team: final name and domain; final prices; which launch cities to seed; whose voice is in the video.
